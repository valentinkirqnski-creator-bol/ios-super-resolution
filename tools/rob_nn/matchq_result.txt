same architecture, same training schedule. The ONLY difference is whether

is exactly equivalent to their not existing). Both models swept to the SAME
visible-false-acceptance budget, so neither is judged at an operating point

THE COLUMN THAT WAS STUCK. Detailed-static retention read 8.3 / 10.3 / 8.9 /
0.1 across a symmetric loss, an asymmetric margin loss, 1728 records of real
static aliasing, a degraded prior and no prior at all -- invariant to
everything tried. With match quality it reads 28.3% at the strictest budget
and 57.8-72.2% at the looser ones. The elimination was right: the constraint
was the FEATURES, and this is the missing evidence.

WHY IT WORKS, which is the part worth keeping. Aliasing changes the residual
at the BOTTOM of the block-matching cost surface without flattening it, so a
correctly aligned but heavily aliased tile still has a sharp, isolated
minimum. A tile matched onto similar-looking content elsewhere does not. The
photometric channels see only the residual and therefore cannot separate those
two cases at all; the cost-surface SHAPE separates them directly. That is why
this succeeded where more data, a different prior and a different loss all
failed.

Note also the gate values. The match-quality model reaches the 1% budget at a
gate of 0.40 rather than 0.98, i.e. it is no longer relying on the extreme top
of its range to be safe. That is a healthier operating point and it means the
mask's mid-range is now carrying real information.

CAVEAT ON ABSOLUTE NUMBERS. This dataset is 4270 records with burst7 at 27% of
it, against the earlier 9148 with burst7 at 19%, so the absolute figures are
not comparable with anything reported before the index fix. The controlled
comparison above is, because both arms share the dataset exactly.
