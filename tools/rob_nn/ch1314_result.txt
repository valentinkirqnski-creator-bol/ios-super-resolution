Removing channels 13-14 does not lift the curve. It collapses the model.

Matched to the symmetric baseline's own misalignment rate, corrected index:

                              safe kept   burst7 static kept
                              bursts1-6   flat  moderate detailed  ALL
  symmetric @0.989               58.5%   69.4%    39.1%     8.3%  52.7%
  no ch13/14 @0.99890             7.2%   12.4%     2.9%     0.1%   7.6%

Every column falls, and by a lot. Detailed-static retention -- the aliasing
case this was aimed at -- goes to 0.1%, i.e. essentially everything is
rejected.

So the ablation was read the wrong way round, and it is worth being explicit
about the mistake. Zeroing channel 13 collapsed detailed R from 0.731 to
0.325, which I took as "the model leans on a blind statistic". The correct
reading is that channel 13 carries most of the usable signal there is: remove
it and almost nothing is left to decide with. A large ablation delta means a
channel is LOAD-BEARING; it does not say whether what it bears is right.

The prior is not a harmful shortcut. It is the evidence.

What this does establish is where the real constraint sits. Channel 15, the
flow residual against the local 3x3 median, was supposed to be the geometric
evidence that separates aliasing from misalignment, and ablating it changes
nothing (+0.0075). That is not because the model ignores good evidence out of
laziness -- it is because on these bursts the flow field is genuinely rough:
measured earlier, the local flow span M has a median of 11-27 RAW PIXELS and
deviation from a global affine model runs to 5-20 px and is CORRECT, being
real parallax from handheld translation over a scene with depth. A residual
that large and that legitimate carries little signal about whether any
particular tile is wrong.

So the binding constraint is the geometric FEATURES, not the photometric prior
and not parameter count. Separating aliasing from misalignment needs evidence
that says where the flow is wrong, and the current flow-consistency channels
cannot say it on a scene with real parallax.

THE INTERMEDIATE, which distinguishes "harmful prior" from "shortcut the
optimiser takes because it is cheap": keep channels 13-14 but corrupt them
with Gaussian noise (sd 1.5 in normalised units) during training, forcing the
model to build a decision that cannot rely on them.

  all matched to the symmetric baseline's misalignment rate (~0.25% visible FA)

  asym+burst7    @0.99820     61.5%    61.4%    29.5%    10.3%   44.7%
  noisy ch13/14  @0.99815     37.7%    37.3%    14.2%     8.9%   25.8%
  no ch13/14     @0.99890      7.2%    12.4%     2.9%     0.1%    7.6%

Degrading the prior degrades the model smoothly and monotonically: full prior
> noised prior > no prior. If it were a shortcut, denying it would have pushed
the model onto better evidence and recovered; instead there is no better
evidence to be pushed onto. That settles it -- the prior is load-bearing, not
a crutch.

THE COLUMN THAT MATTERS MOST is detailed-static retention, the aliasing case:
8.3%, 10.3%, 8.9%, 0.1%. Across a symmetric loss, an asymmetric margin loss,
1728 records of real static aliasing, a corrupted prior and no prior at all,
it does not move above ~10%. Nine of ten provably-safe detailed pixels are
discarded no matter what is changed about the prior, the objective or the
data.

That is the signature of a FEATURE limitation, and it is consistent with the
one measurement that explains it: the geometric channels cannot indicate which
tile is wrong on a scene with real parallax, because the honest flow field is
itself rough to 11-27 raw px locally and 5-20 px against a global model.

To move that column, the mask needs geometric evidence it does not currently
have -- a residual measured against a LOCAL motion model rather than a median,
or a match-quality statistic carried out of the aligner (the block-matching
cost of the chosen offset against the runner-up, which is already computed and
thrown away, and which says directly whether a tile's match was unique).
Nothing about the loss or the prior will substitute for it.
