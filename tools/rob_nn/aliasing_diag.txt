Over-rejection in static scenes is ALIASING CONFUSION, and the ablation says
which channel is doing it.

burst7 is a static scene with real hand tremor: real aliasing, real noise,
zero misalignment. Every pixel is ground-truth safe, so every rejection below
is a FALSE rejection. Split by local contrast in units of sensor sigma:

  model        region     px%   mean R   >0.989   rejected(<0.5)
  symmetric    flat      51.1%   0.949    55.9%        2.0%
  symmetric    moderate  41.5%   0.877    10.9%        4.3%
  symmetric    detailed   7.4%   0.731     0.7%       17.7%
  asymmetric   flat      51.1%   0.895    82.8%       11.3%
  asymmetric   moderate  41.5%   0.754    53.9%       25.6%
  asymmetric   detailed   7.4%   0.529    27.2%       50.4%

Rejection scales monotonically with DETAIL on a scene where nothing is
misaligned. That is the signature of aliasing being read as misalignment --
detail is exactly where sub-pixel lattice differences between frames are
largest.

Both failures are present and they are distinguishable:
  * aliasing confusion: detailed 0.7% above the gate against flat 55.9%
  * under-confidence:  even FLAT static pixels only reach 55.9% above 0.989

ABLATION (replace a channel by its training mean, symmetric model, burst7):

           ablated      mean R all   mean R detailed   rejected detailed
   none (baseline)         0.9028            0.7310             17.7%
   ch13 log(d2/s2)         0.7757            0.3250             82.6%
   ch14 analytic R         0.8827            0.6328             31.8%
      ch13+14 both         0.7839            0.3452             84.4%
   ch6-8 comp mean         0.8719            0.6822             24.4%
ch15 flow residual         0.9061            0.7385             16.7%
        ch17 ref HF        0.9025            0.6929             24.5%

The decision is dominated by channel 13, log1p(d^2/sigma^2) -- the photometric
statistic that CANNOT separate aliasing from misalignment, since both show up
only as a colour difference. Meanwhile channel 15, the flow residual against
the local median, is the geometric evidence that CAN separate them, and
ablating it changes almost nothing (+0.0075): the model is barely using it.

So the model is deciding with the one statistic that is blind to the
distinction and ignoring the one that is not. That is a features/objective
problem, not a parameter-count problem, and it is why more capacity would not
have helped.
